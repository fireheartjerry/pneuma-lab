\begin{abstract}
When a coding agent succeeds after receiving verifier feedback, the natural
reading is that the feedback helped. Three cheaper explanations survive that
observation: the agent would have succeeded on any continuation from the same
state, a plausible-looking report of the wrong findings would have worked just
as well, and the apparent gain is inside the noise that stochastic decoding
produces between two identical continuations. Placebo, shape-matched, and
compute-matched controls are established tools for separating these, and we
claim no novelty for them. We claim their composition and enforcement in a
setting where they have not been applied. We describe \emph{the PLACEBO
Protocol}---Preregistered, Label-blind, Arm-controlled, Causal Evaluation of
Behavioral Outcomes---and preregister \emph{the PLACEBO Trial}, its first
application. From one frozen prefix per task, taken at an exact tool boundary,
the protocol branches four continuations under identical budgets: a correct
task-specific verifier packet (\texttt{REAL}); a shape- and
tokenizer-length-matched packet derived from a different task
(\texttt{SHAM}); and two exchangeable no-feedback replicates
(\texttt{NONE}, \texttt{RESAMPLE}) whose paired disagreement is measured rather
than assumed to be zero. Co-primary estimands are the content effect
$\Delta_{\text{content}}$ and the causal excess $\Delta_{\text{excess}}$, both
of which must clear a resolution floor estimated from the no-feedback pair
itself. Outcomes are objective binary endpoints in two materially different
environments---repository repair and stateful tool/API dialogue. Arm labels
never reach the analyst before a hash-gated unblind ceremony, thresholds and
verdict rules are sealed beforehand, and negative and unresolved verdicts are
publishable by construction. This manuscript reports the protocol and its
falsification conditions. \result{no arm outcome exists at submission of this
draft}
\end{abstract}

\section{The observation that is not evidence}
\label{sec:intro}

An agent works on a repository issue, runs the test suite, receives a report
of failing assertions, revises its patch, and passes. Reported as an ablation,
this is a verifier effect. It is also consistent with at least three
alternatives that cost nothing to propose and are rarely measured.

\textbf{Another attempt.} The agent received a continuation, not just
information. Sampling four more tokens from a state that was one revision away
from correct sometimes succeeds regardless of what was in the intervening
message. Any comparison of ``feedback'' against ``stop here'' confounds
evidence with an additional opportunity.

\textbf{Form without content.} A verifier packet is an interruption, a
structured artifact, and an implicit instruction to reconsider. Work on
prompting and on retrieval has repeatedly found that the shape of an
intervention carries effect independent of whether its content is correct
\cite{min_rethinkingdemonstrations_2022,cuconasu_powerofnoise_2024}. An agent
handed a well-formed report of the wrong task's failures may still stop,
re-read its diff, and fix the bug it already knew about.

\textbf{Noise that looks like signal.} Two continuations from an identical
state with independent seeds disagree on binary task success at a rate that is
a property of the model, the scaffold, and the task---not of any intervention.
Recent agent-evaluation work has made this concrete: small task counts and
single runs produce differences that do not survive repetition
\cite{card_littlepower_2020,miller_errorbars_2024,bjarnason_randomness_2026,huang_howmanytasks_2026}.
Unless that rate is measured on the same tasks under the same conditions, a
five-point gain has no denominator.

Controls for all three exist and are not ours. Clinical methodology has used
sham interventions and preregistration for decades \cite{ich_e10_2000}, and
2026 work in small-scale code repair already tests placebo, shape-matched, and
compute-matched controls
\cite{iscan_falsificationnotexposure_2026,iscan_formnotcontent_2026,iscan_scaffoldnotvocabulary_2026}.
Our contribution is not the idea of a control. It is a protocol that composes
all three, enforces them mechanically, and applies them where they have not
been applied: long-horizon interactive agents whose intervention point is a
tool boundary deep inside a trajectory rather than a single generation.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The PLACEBO Protocol}: a four-arm, snapshot-paired design
    that separates correct task-specific verifier content from a mismatched
    report and from pooled no-feedback continuation, at an exact tool boundary
    in a long-horizon trajectory (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).
    \item \textbf{A measured resolution floor}: the two no-feedback replicates
    are not a diagnostic, they are the yardstick. Their label-swapped contrast
    distribution supplies $r_{95}$, and no effect below it is reportable
    (\S\ref{sec:inference}).
    \item \textbf{Objective cross-domain outcomes}: binary endpoints from
    repository repair and from stateful tool/API dialogue, weighted equally and
    never pooled as one exchangeable population (\S\ref{sec:environments}).
    \item \textbf{An arm-blind sealed evidence contract}: capability separation
    across power, scheduling, assignment, execution, and analysis, with a
    hash-gated single-use unblind ceremony (\S\ref{sec:blinding}).
    \item \textbf{Preregistered verdicts including the negative ones}: seven
    verdicts, all publishable, none permitting an endpoint change
    (\S\ref{sec:verdicts}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Closest prior art, and what is left}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Placebo and shape-matched controls in code tasks.}
Three 2026 studies isolate exposure from falsification, form from content, and
scaffold from vocabulary in small code-repair settings
\cite{iscan_falsificationnotexposure_2026,iscan_formnotcontent_2026,iscan_scaffoldnotvocabulary_2026}.
They establish that a shape-matched control is necessary and that form alone
moves outcomes. They operate on short single-file tasks with one generation per
condition. PLACEBO inherits their control logic and moves it to trajectories
with tens of tool calls, where the intervention point itself must be defined,
the pre-intervention state must be held byte-identical across arms, and an
additional continuation is itself a treatment.

\paragraph{Retry, resampling, and prefix reuse.}
Recent work on agent reinforcement learning localises an erroneous turn and
retries from that state, reusing the correct prefix
\cite{arl_pivotal_retry_2026}, and error-attribution work backtracks to a step
and resamples from a bare prefix as an untargeted retry
\cite{reflect_error_attribution_2026}. These treat prefix-anchored resampling
as a \emph{method} for improving or diagnosing agents. PLACEBO treats it as a
\emph{control}: the resampled branch is not an intervention to be improved but
the measuring stick against which an intervention is judged. The distinction
matters because a method paper has no reason to characterise the resampling
distribution, and a control paper has no other job.

\paragraph{Unreliable and adversarial feedback.}
Work on how unreliable tool feedback degrades agents
\cite{unreliable_feedback_2026} establishes that wrong information is harmful.
PLACEBO's \texttt{SHAM} arm asks the adjacent question: whether a
\emph{plausible but task-mismatched} report is distinguishable from a correct
one in outcome, when both are matched on schema, field count, severity
distribution, and exact tokenizer length. We explicitly do not claim sham
feedback is inert; $\Delta_{\text{sham\_packet}}$ is reported as its own
quantity.

\paragraph{Prior art we must reconcile before submission.}
A July 2026 paper, ``Try Again, Don't Look Back'', was identified as closest
prior art for the retry-versus-feedback contrast. Its bibliographic record
could not be resolved at the time of drafting; it sits in the citation
verification queue at
\texttt{paper/placebo/citation-queue.json} and the paper preflight blocks while
any queue entry is unresolved. \TODO{resolve the ``Try Again, Don't Look Back''
citation and write the explicit delta paragraph against it}

\paragraph{Measurement reliability of agent benchmarks.}
Paired noise floors, resolution diagnostics, and harness variance are active
concerns
\cite{kotawala_resolutiondiagnostics_2026,bowyer_dontusetheclt_2025,siska_robustness_2024,kaliyev_noisefloor_2026}.
PLACEBO's difference is that the floor is estimated \emph{inside the same
randomisation}, from the same tasks, at the same trigger, under the same
budgets, rather than from a separate repeated-run study whose conditions may not
transfer.

\paragraph{Candidate novelty, stated narrowly.}
To our knowledge, the conjunction of (i) long-horizon interactive tool agents,
(ii) exact snapshot-paired branching from one frozen prefix, (iii)
mismatched-verifier shams matched on shape and tokenizer length, (iv) an
empirically measured resampling resolution floor, (v) objective outcomes across
two materially different domains, (vi) preregistered inference with sealed
thresholds, and (vii) arm-blind sealed evidence publication has not previously
been applied together. Each element separately is prior art.

\section{The PLACEBO Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Unit, trigger, and snapshot}

The randomised causal unit is one benchmark task definition. Seeds and branches
are repeated measurements nested inside the task; repository is the highest
lineage for repair tasks, and domain plus issue family are additional blocks
for dialogue tasks.

The intervention trigger is the earliest completed tool boundary satisfying
either (a) the first mutating action has returned and the state is
verifier-eligible, or (b) the fourth subject tool call has returned. The
trigger fires only after a tool result is committed; it never interrupts a
command, a transaction, or a model response.

At the trigger the controller records and hashes the task and benchmark
revisions, the complete arm-visible transcript, the subject sampling state and
remaining quotas, and the tool call/result ledger. This snapshot is created
\emph{once per task}. All four arms continue from the same bytes, so an arm
difference cannot be attributed to a different pre-intervention trajectory.

Tasks that terminate before a trigger form a fixed-denominator
\texttt{no\_intervention\_opportunity} stratum. They are retained in the primary
intention-to-treat analysis with all four outcomes equal to the terminal prefix
grade, contributing zero contrast. They are not discarded, re-run with a
different trigger, or used to select a more favourable subject.
Trigger-conditional effects are preregistered secondary estimates and cannot
replace the fixed-roster result.

\subsection{Four arms}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{The four arms. Every arm receives identical post-trigger caps on model
calls, generated tokens, tool calls, wall clock, disk, and network. There is no
best-of selection; the endpoint is the direct result of the single
continuation.}
\label{tab:arms}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
arm & injected evidence & continuation & role \\
\midrule
\texttt{REAL}     & correct task-specific verifier packet & one IID slot & treatment \\
\texttt{SHAM}     & shape/length-matched packet from another lineage & one IID slot & content control \\
\texttt{NONE}     & none & one IID slot & no-feedback replicate \\
\texttt{RESAMPLE} & none & one IID slot & no-feedback replicate \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For each frozen prefix the controller creates four branch slots with
independent preregistered seed streams and a randomised execution-order and
hardware schedule. A uniform within-task draw assigns the multiset
$\{\texttt{REAL}, \texttt{SHAM}, \texttt{NO\_PACKET}, \texttt{NO\_PACKET}\}$
over the twelve possible slot allocations, and a fair coin labels the two
no-packet slots \texttt{NONE} and \texttt{RESAMPLE} solely for the resampling
audit. Both draws occur before any continuation runs, are derived from a
committed master key through a typed binary key-derivation frame, and are
sealed in an allocation receipt that a verifier can reconstruct independently.
The allocation never depends on a branch outcome.

\texttt{REAL} and \texttt{SHAM} have \emph{exactly} equal subject-token length
under the frozen tokenizer, packet schema, field count, and severity
distribution: bounded evidence is deterministically truncated and neutral
padding is added by an identical algorithm on both sides. They differ in
whether the findings apply to the focal task.

We do not pretend the no-payload arms have the same input length as the packet
arms. $\Delta_{\text{sham\_packet}}$ therefore measures the net effect of a
mismatched report---extra prompt tokens, misinformation cost, and the generic
instruction to reconsider---and is not a pure apparatus estimand. Isolating
packet form alone would require a fifth neutral-format arm and is outside this
study.

\subsection{Verifier and sham construction}

The real verifier runs against a disposable clone of the prefix state. For
repair tasks the packet may report per-check pass/fail, a normalised
compiler/assertion/runtime failure class, a bounded log excerpt, regression
status, and resource or timeout status; it may not contain test source, the
gold patch, future commits, or an unbounded traceback. For dialogue tasks it
reports eligible-component type and satisfaction, a policy-safe reason code,
the relevant action or communication category, and bounded current-state
evidence; expected finals, hidden answers, gold actions and arguments,
assertion values, and database diffs remain controller-only. No language model
and no network call participates in the primary verifier.

Sham donors are assigned by a deterministic constrained minimum-cost perfect
matching over triggered tasks: exactly one donor per focal task and one focal
per donor, no self-edge, no same-lineage edge, and no two-cycle. Candidates are
matched on language or domain, failure class, failure-count band, and
report-length band, and must come from a different repository or task family.
Ties are broken by an HMAC over a typed frame, so the matching is reproducible
from the receipts. Before confirmation, every real finding must validate
against its objective verifier artifact, every sham packet must be false or
irrelevant for its focal prefix under a collision checker, and a blinded packet
classifier must have cross-validated AUROC whose upper 95\% task-lineage
bootstrap bound is below 0.60. Failure is \texttt{packet\_invalid}, which blocks
confirmation rather than inviting a rewrite after outcomes are visible.

\section{Environments, subject, and outcomes}
\label{sec:environments}

\paragraph{Environments.}
Repository repair uses the MultiLang split of a live, continuously refreshed
repair benchmark across eight languages, pinned to immutable harness, dataset,
and launcher revisions. Stateful tool/API work uses the objective text subset
of a conversational agent benchmark at a pinned tag, restricted to tasks with
deterministic evaluators: all natural-language-assertion and
language-model-judged components are excluded, so every endpoint is decided by
a program. Domains, languages, and issue families are blocking factors. The two
environments receive equal weight and are never pooled as one exchangeable
population.

\paragraph{Subject.}
One frozen open-weight mixture-of-experts model at a pinned revision, served in
its official FP8 form under a pinned inference stack with a pinned tokenizer,
chat template, reasoning parser, and tool-call parser. A BF16 run is a
\emph{different subject} and may appear only as a separately reported
precision/provider replication; the two are never pooled. A byte-equality
reproducibility gate---fixed execution order, fixed concurrency, batch-invariant
kernels, per-call seeds---must pass, or serving is declared infeasible rather
than accepted with caveats. Topology is frozen as a four-rung candidate ladder
with a deterministic selection rule that may never consult efficacy, arm
effects, or any outcome signal.

\paragraph{Outcome.}
The primary outcome is the benchmark's objective binary endpoint: for repair,
all registered target and regression checks pass exactly once in a clean
endpoint image; for dialogue, all eligible objective components pass under the
pinned evaluator configuration. Missing, skipped, unparsed, or duplicated checks
fail closed.

An arm-specific infrastructure or model failure scores zero. A preregistered,
arm-blind provider-outage receipt created \emph{before} any endpoint is readable
may trigger one full four-arm rerun with identical snapshot, seeds, and
allocation; if that rerun cannot complete, all four outcomes are zero through a
fail-closed finaliser that never calls the endpoint grader. No allocated task is
ever excluded or replaced. Let $f_a$ be the equal-benchmark-weighted
infrastructure-failure rate of arm $a$; the registered gate is
$\max_{a,a'}|f_a - f_{a'}| \le 0.02$. Larger imbalance marks the pipeline
invalid---it never licenses deleting a task.

Secondary outcomes include native partial reward, target-test gain and
regression creation, evaluator-component gain, completion and refusal and
malformed-action and timeout and cap-binding rates, resource counters,
post-intervention state-edit distance and action-path divergence, and packet
acknowledgement and cited-finding rates.

\section{Estimands, inference, and the resolution floor}
\label{sec:inference}

\subsection{Estimands}

Let $Y_R, Y_S, Y_N, Y_Z$ be task success under \texttt{REAL}, \texttt{SHAM},
\texttt{NONE}, \texttt{RESAMPLE}, and let $Y_0$ be the disposable prefix-state
grade. With $\text{no\_feedback} = (Y_N + Y_Z)/2$:
\begin{align*}
\Delta_{\text{content}} &= Y_R - Y_S
&\Delta_{\text{excess}} &= Y_R - \text{no\_feedback}\\
\Delta_{\text{sham\_packet}} &= Y_S - \text{no\_feedback}
&\Delta_{\text{continuation}} &= \text{no\_feedback} - Y_0\\
\Delta_{\text{total}} &= Y_R - Y_0
&\Delta_{\text{null}} &= Y_Z - Y_N
\end{align*}

$\Delta_{\text{content}}$ and $\Delta_{\text{excess}}$ are co-primary; both must
clear every registered gate. The other three are required decompositions, not
optional diagnostics. The cross-setting estimator gives each environment weight
one half and averages within environment over its fixed roster; per-environment
estimates are always reported.

\subsection{The resolution floor}

\texttt{NONE} and \texttt{RESAMPLE} are exchangeable no-payload replicates on
IID frozen slots. With $D_{bi} = Y_{biZ} - Y_{biN}$ and weights
$w_{bi} = 1/(2n_b)$, the protocol reports the observed per-task discordance
$q_0 = \sum_{bi} w_{bi}|D_{bi}|$ and the label-swapped contrast scale
\[
    r_{95} = \inf\Bigl\{ r : \Pr_\epsilon\bigl[\, |{\textstyle\sum_{bi}} w_{bi}\,\epsilon_{bi} D_{bi}| \le r \,\bigr] \ge 0.95 \Bigr\},
    \qquad \epsilon_{bi} \in \{-1,+1\} \text{ independently.}
\]
The sign-flip distribution is exact under exchangeability of the two no-payload
labels. Neither $q_0$ nor $r_{95}$ is a confidence interval or a minimum
detectable effect. $\Delta_{\text{null}}$ is a randomised-label balance
diagnostic only; there is deliberately no mean-equivalence gate, because
cancellation could let one pass under maximal unit-level instability.

\subsection{Tests and gates}

Exact Fisher tests are reported for sharp unit-level nulls, using the
within-task randomisation that generated the allocation; where enumeration is
infeasible, a valid Monte Carlo Fisher test uses $999{,}999$ digest-bound draws
with add-one $p$-values and a reported Monte Carlo standard error. Because the
scientific alternative is the intersection
$\{\tau_{\text{content}} > 0 \wedge \tau_{\text{excess}} > 0\}$, the sharp-null
decision is an intersection--union test and needs no multiplicity correction
\cite{berger_intersectionunion_1982}. Average-effect inference uses a
benchmark-stratified, task-cluster Romano--Wolf multiplier max-$t$ procedure
with $99{,}999$ frozen Rademacher draws, giving simultaneous one-sided lower
bounds $L_k$. These bounds are asymptotically valid and Neyman-conservative
under independent randomised task blocks; they are not finite-sample exact, and
the paper will not describe them as though they were.

A positive claim requires \emph{all} of: both local Fisher $p$-values at most
$0.05$; both simultaneous lower bounds above zero; both point estimates at least
the practical screen $\delta^\star = 0.05$; both point estimates strictly above
$r_{95}$; non-negative point estimates in each environment separately; a
leave-one-group-out sensitivity of at least $-0.05$ for every preregistered
group; and the differential-failure gate satisfied. $\delta^\star$ is a
preregistered observed-effect screen, not a confidence claim that either effect
is at least five percentage points; that stronger statement is licensed only if
the simultaneous lower bounds themselves exceed $0.05$.

\subsection{Power and roster}

A deterministic pre-outcome power simulation evaluates the two candidate roster
tiers---120 and 160 tasks per environment---against a frozen alternative of
$0.15$ on both co-primary estimands, over a nuisance grid crossing baseline
success, trigger rate, and latent equicorrelation. The registered requirement is
joint power at least $0.80$ in every alternative cell with familywise type-I
error at most $0.05$ across the null-boundary cells. The tier is selected by
that simulation on the frozen eligible roster \emph{before} the schedule seed is
revealed and before any prefix is created; a synthetic roster validates code and
runtime only and can never select a tier. If the eligible roster cannot meet the
minimum tier, the verdict is \texttt{FEASIBILITY\_NO\_GO}; there is no smaller
anecdotal fallback.

At the time of writing, roster feasibility is externally unverified for both
tiers and both environments, and remains a blocking gate.

\section{Blinding, authority, and the evidence contract}
\label{sec:blinding}

Capability separation is enforced, not requested. The power process sees
manifest authority, the nuisance grid, and topology, and never an endpoint. The
scheduling process sees the revealed schedule seed, manifest, and completed
power final, and never the assignment master key, verifier output, arms, or
outcomes. The assignment process reads the master key only after prefixes and
verifier artifacts are frozen, and cannot read branch outcomes. Workers receive
one opaque per-slot capability plus the frozen snapshot, seeds, and caps---never
an arm label, a donor identity, the ledger, or another branch. Raw packets are
encrypted; a worker decrypts only its own slot's packet.

The analyst receives a blinded projection stripped to success, prefix success,
finite reward, an infrastructure bit, and resource counters. The projection
forbids references, paths, packets, grades, sources, arms, donors, keys, and
hidden identities. Unblinding is a single ceremony: a hash-gated unblinder
consumes a single-use secret handle, verifies the commitment and context,
derives exactly one subkey, and recomputes a framed permit HMAC over the study
identifier, manifest, schedule, prefix index, ledger, projection, analysis
freeze, and expected task count \emph{before} parsing the ledger. The analyst
holds no unblinding key until the analysis source and its expected outputs are
sealed. The first post-unblind run is the record; corrections append a deviation
artifact and never overwrite.

Three values---a roster nonce, the schedule seed, and the assignment master
key---are committed separately before ranking and before any prefix executes,
with the commitment externally timestamped ahead of a public randomness beacon
round. The final roster seed derives from the precommit, the revealed nonce, and
the authenticated beacon output, so the roster cannot be reshuffled after the
fact.

Interruption is treated as a first-class failure mode. Every completed model
response and tool result is content-addressed, uploaded, hash-verified, and
followed by an atomic completion marker before the next boundary; a local
marker alone is insufficient. A resume must match its prior boundary receipt,
and a mismatch invalidates the entire four-arm block rather than silently
replacing one arm.

\section{Verdicts, failure handling, and what would falsify us}
\label{sec:verdicts}

Seven verdicts are preregistered, and all seven are publishable. None permits
changing an endpoint or deleting a failed block.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Preregistered verdicts. The taxonomy is fixed before execution, so a
disappointing result cannot be reclassified into a better one.}
\label{tab:verdicts}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
verdict & trigger \\
\midrule
\texttt{FEASIBILITY\_NO\_GO}     & before outcomes: the powered roster or runtime cannot complete \\
\texttt{PIPELINE\_INVALID}       & a packet, snapshot, assignment, interference, grader, or failure gate fails \\
\texttt{HARMFUL\_OR\_MISDIRECTING} & a co-primary estimate $\le -0.05$ with simultaneous upper bound $< 0$ \\
\texttt{CAUSAL\_CONTENT}         & both co-primary contrasts clear every registered gate \\
\texttt{SHAM\_PACKET\_ONLY}      & the sham effect clears its gates while content fails at least one \\
\texttt{UNRESOLVED\_RESAMPLING}  & a co-primary estimate fails $\delta^\star$ or $r_{95}$, or a standard error is invalid \\
\texttt{RESAMPLING\_CONSISTENT}  & valid and resolved, and the real-feedback gains fail the registered controls \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\texttt{UNRESOLVED\_RESAMPLING} is the verdict this design exists to make
possible. An effect can have a small conventional $p$-value against a
no-feedback baseline and still sit below the disagreement rate between two
identical no-feedback continuations. Reporting such an effect as a verifier
benefit is the specific error the protocol is built to prevent, and the gate
fires regardless of how the result reads.

\paragraph{Stopping rules.} Execution stops or narrows before confirmation if
roster gates fail; if a mutable or digest-free container image, an isolation
escape, or an evaluator network call occurs; if the trigger opportunity rate
falls below 60\%; if gold or regression flakiness exceeds 2\%; if any
arm-visible snapshot differs in bytes, tokens, or state; if a real-correctness,
sham-collision, or detectability gate fails; if differential infrastructure
failure exceeds two percentage points; if throughput cannot finish by the
registered date; if the power simulation misses 80\% at the minimum tier; or if
verified funding misses the exact worst case. With zero available credit the
fallback is a methods-and-feasibility report using the validated controller and
a clearly labelled small-model pilot---not the powered cross-environment
result.

\paragraph{Explicit non-claims.} PLACEBO does not show that verification helps
every model or scaffold; does not identify the effect of a human expert
verifier; does not assert sham feedback is semantically inert; does not identify
packet form alone; does not treat seeds, trajectories, issues from one
repository, or dialogue permutations as independent scientific units; and does
not claim exact prompt-token equality between packet and no-packet arms.

\section{Reproducibility, limitations, and ethics}
\label{sec:repro}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every number in the results version of this paper will be emitted by a pipeline
that accepts only a verified sealed evidence package and refuses a manual entry.
The pipeline rejects an incomplete or unsealed artifact root, a package produced
under the wrong authority, a missing receipt, and any unresolved placeholder in
the manuscript. Table and figure sources, the claim--evidence matrix, and the
artifact appendix are generated, not written. The analysis code, its expected
outputs, and the roster are hash-bound before unblinding.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Inference is conditional on the realised prefixes and measures post-trigger
continuation variation, not whole-run randomness or an unrestricted population
of unseen repositories. One subject at one precision on one serving stack
supports no cross-model statement. The dialogue subset excludes every
language-model-judged component, which narrows what ``task success'' means
there. The finite-roster estimand is a statement about the frozen roster.
Provider placement is not randomised into the primary effect; a different
kernel, precision, or provider is a separate replication block. Simultaneous
average-effect bounds are asymptotic. Roster feasibility is unverified at the
time of writing and may force a tier reduction or a no-go.

\paragraph{Ethics and compute.}
No human subjects and no personal data. All environments and model weights are
public and used under their stated licences; upstream repository contents are
not thereby relicensed. Compute is budgeted with registered per-tier caps and a
watchdog that meters every billing dimension; the study has no authority to
exceed a cap and no result depends on doing so.

\section{Conclusion}

A verifier that appears to help may be supplying evidence, supplying an
interruption, or supplying nothing but another draw from the same distribution.
Distinguishing these requires controls that are not novel and an enforcement
discipline that is rarely applied to long-horizon agents. The PLACEBO Protocol
composes a matched sham, a paired no-feedback replicate, a measured resolution
floor, sealed thresholds, and arm-blind publication into one contract, and
commits in advance to reporting the verdict it produces---including the verdict
that the question could not be resolved at this resolution.
